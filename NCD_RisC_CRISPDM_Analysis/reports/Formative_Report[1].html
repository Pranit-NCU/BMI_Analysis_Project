1. Business Understanding

Introduction

This report investigates the relationship between obesity and diabetes prevalence across countries and over time, with a particular focus on understanding gender differences. Using data from the Non-Communicable Disease Risk Factor Collaboration (NCD-RisC), we explore how these two critical health risk factors have evolved from 1990 to 2022.

Research Questions

The analysis addresses the following key research questions:

  1. How does obesity prevalence compare between countries over time?
  2. Is there a relationship between obesity and diabetes prevalence globally?
  3. Do gender differences exist in the obesity-diabetes relationship?
  4. What trends can we observe in these health risk factors from 1990 to 2022?

Key Performance Indicators (KPIs)

  • Prevalence of obesity (BMI ≥ 30 kg/m²): Percentage of population classified as obese
  • Prevalence of diabetes (age 18+ years): Age-standardized percentage with diabetes diagnosis
  • Temporal trends: Changes in prevalence over 1990–2022
  • Gender comparisons: Sex-stratified analysis for Men and Women
  • Country comparisons: Focus on India, United Kingdom, and global aggregates

Motivation

Understanding obesity and diabetes trends is critical for public health planning, policy development, and resource allocation. These conditions are major risk factors for cardiovascular disease, cancers, and other non-communicable diseases (NCDs). By examining trends and relationships, we can inform prevention strategies and identify high-risk populations.

2. Data Understanding

Data Sources

This analysis uses two datasets from the NCD-RisC collaboration:

  1. BMI Dataset (NCD_RisC_Lancet_2024_BMI_age_standardised_country.csv)
    • Contains age-standardized prevalence of BMI categories
    • Covers years 1990–2022
    • Stratified by sex (Men, Women)
    • Country-level and global aggregates
  2. Diabetes Dataset (NCD_RisC_Lancet_2024_Diabetes_age_standardised_countries.csv)
    • Contains age-standardized diabetes prevalence (18+ years)
    • Covers years 1990–2022
    • Stratified by sex (Men, Women)
    • Country-level and global aggregates

Investigative Conclusion

The linear regression model evaluated the relationship between obesity prevalence and diabetes prevalence across countries and years, accounting for sex as an additional predictor. The model found that higher obesity prevalence is significantly associated with increased diabetes prevalence: for each unit increase in the prevalence of obesity, diabetes prevalence rises on average by approximately 0.31 percentage points, with a highly significant p-value (<2×10−16<2×10−16).

The coefficient for sex (Women) is negative and statistically significant, indicating that, after adjusting for obesity, women tend to have a lower diabetes prevalence than men. The intercept is also significant, representing baseline diabetes prevalence when other predictors are zero.

While the model fits the data well overall (all predictors highly significant), the Multiple R-squared (0.48) shows that about 48% of the variation in diabetes prevalence is explained by obesity and sex alone. This suggests that other factors—such as genetics, lifestyle, and health system variables—play a substantial role. The residual standard error and patterns in residuals further support the need for more complex models or additional predictors for full understanding.

In summary: The model confirms obesity and sex are both important predictors of diabetes prevalence globally, but much of the variation in diabetes prevalence cannot be explained by these factors alone. A more nuanced investigation including other risk factors is recommended for a deeper understanding.
